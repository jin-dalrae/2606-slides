## Multi-voice debate already exists — the map does not

<p class="slide-ref">Act 1 · Opportunity</p>

Reddit, forums, and chat hold competing views, sources, and threads.

On a phone or browser, order is mostly rank or time. Related positions, bridges, and gaps stay hard to see as a whole.

People can scroll for a long time and still not know where the debate sits.

<p class="slide-closing">The opportunity is orientation: help people explore ideas, not only consume a stream.</p>

## Quiet reading is a success path

<p class="slide-ref">Act 2 · Secondary</p>

Community research treats non-posting as participation — learning norms, gathering context, deciding when to speak.

For Cosmos that means:

- Do not score the product by post rate first  
- Design for scan, inspect, and return  
- Contribution comes after reading already works  

<p class="slide-closing">Source trail: /cosmos/secondary/ · quiet-reading chapter and linked literature.</p>

## Spatial cues help attention — live voice can wait

<p class="slide-ref">Act 2 · Secondary</p>

Spatial communications work shows location can help people separate overlapping signals.

That supports place as an attention cue for a wall of posts.

It does **not** require Cosmos to start as a live spatial voice room. Live voice is expensive socially and technically; the first product is async browsing.

<p class="slide-closing">Detail: /cosmos/secondary/spatial-communications/</p>

## Arrangement systems already tried structure as layout

<p class="slide-ref">Act 2 · Secondary</p>

From Memex trails through Project Xanadu to spatial hypertext and modern boards, computing has repeatedly used place to organize knowledge.

What we take:

- Layout can encode relation and parallel views  
- Source-linked structure matters  

What we avoid:

- Making every reader hand-author a graph  
- Illegible free layout with no named rule  

<p class="slide-closing">Detail: /cosmos/secondary/spatial-organization/</p>

## Kris: multi-source orientation beats phone-parity

<p class="slide-ref">Act 2 · Primary</p>

For casual single-feed scroll, the phone already wins on setup and habit.

What spatial form can earn: several sources or stances visible together, so someone can stay oriented across a topic.

That only works if the field is not chaotic — too much full text at once collapses the advantage.

<p class="slide-closing">Design response: multi-source / multi-stance jobs + controlled density.</p>

---

## Johnny: focus one idea; keep the rest of the wall

<p class="slide-ref">Act 2 · Primary</p>

Useful exploration needs a clear focus card and a quieter periphery — not every card competing at full strength.

Source trust: every AI label or cluster must open to the posts that produced it.

<p class="slide-closing">Design response: focus isolation + provenance on every generated structure.</p>

---

## Sequence: make exploration work before community scale

<p class="slide-ref">Act 2 · Method</p>

1. Control seed discourse so the wall is studyable  
2. Prove spatial browsing: comprehension, comfort, place memory  
3. Support save, return, and triage while reading  
4. Add light contribution when reading already works  
5. Grow community and optional voice only if earlier phases hold  

<p class="slide-closing">The product people explore first is the wall — not a full social network.</p>

## Exploration needs a layout people can read as a map

<p class="slide-ref">Act 3 · Design</p>

A spatial alternative only works if placement is meaningful.

If a card sits in front of the reader, they should be able to say **why it is there** without a tour.

<p class="slide-closing">Rules and checklist: /cosmos/design-decision/</p>

## Three axes for the default wall

<p class="slide-ref">Act 3 · Placement</p>

| Axis | Role in exploration | Must show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Region** | Topic or debate neighborhood | Labels or legend — not color alone |
| **Depth** | Recency or priority (near / far) | Readable depth cue |
| **Adjacency** | Similarity, bridges, related posts | Link, shared tag, or neighbor affordance |

Inside a region: high / medium / peripheral priority (shown as hypotheses until tested).

<p class="slide-closing">Invisible rules get misread — make the real rules visible.</p>

---

## Focus and density for readable exploration

<p class="slide-ref">Act 3 · Design</p>

**Focus** — one active card; neighbors stay dim and short.  
**Density** — periphery = title + one line; full text only on the active card.  
**Coding** — never color alone for category; use labels, region, and pattern.

<p class="slide-closing">Readable exploration beats a dense sphere of full posts.</p>

---

## Actions the wall must support

<p class="slide-ref">Act 3 · Design</p>

| Action | Result for the reader |
| --- | --- |
| Focus / bring forward | Card becomes the reading surface |
| Save / pin | Return path on a visible shelf |
| Discard / archive | Off the active wall; recoverable |
| Rearrange | By time or by source |
| Open source | Trail from any AI label to posts |
| Leave | One step when done |

<p class="slide-closing">Without these, the wall is a scene — not a tool for exploring ideas.</p>

## What the wall cannot erase

<p class="slide-ref">Act 4 · Stakeholders</p>

Even with a strong wall:

- Hardware cost and comfort still limit who can use headsets  
- Stores and platform policy still shape distribution  
- Empty walls without seed content still fail  
- Chat and feeds still own daily habit  

<p class="slide-closing">Design closes named gaps; it does not pretend structural one-ways disappear.</p>

## Responsibility while we build exploration

<p class="slide-ref">Act 4 · Impact</p>

| Risk | Design response |
| --- | --- |
| Only premium headsets benefit | Keep /web first-class |
| Spatial layout used only to extend session length | Optimize for orientation and clean exit |
| Abuse and steward load at multi-user scale | Tools before heavy contribution |
| Body / voice data exposure | Opt-in; text path always works |
| Empty wall | Seed content before claiming a living community |
| Overclaiming impact | Publish tests and falsifiers |

## What we still need to prove

<p class="slide-ref">Act 5 · Next</p>

- Same discourse: flat feed vs spatial wall  
- Can people find structure and opposing views more clearly?  
- Does place memory help return later?  
- Do strangers read regions and priority the way we intend?  
- Does the experience stay comfortable and leaveable?

<p class="slide-closing">Until those tests run, Cosmos is researchable — not finished.</p>

---

## How we decide whether to continue

<p class="slide-ref">Decision</p>

| Continue if | Narrow or stop if |
| --- | --- |
| Wall helps people explore and understand better than a feed | No gain after novelty |
| People can leave without feeling trapped | Only “works” as longer dwell |
| Desktop and headset paths both matter | Only premium headsets benefit |
| Seeded walls feel like real discourse | Empty space wastes attention |
| Layout rules are readable | Placement still feels arbitrary |

<p class="slide-closing">If the wall does not help understanding, we do not add more social features on top.</p>
