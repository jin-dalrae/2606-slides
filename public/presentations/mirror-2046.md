## The city runs on faces it already knows

<p class="slide-ref">01 · World</p>

A new wave of immigrants has remade the Bay Area's social, legal, and media life. Native-born numbers are small. Birth rates never recovered.

Youth management makes almost everyone look the same age. Age drops out as a public fact.

What remains is labor: hyper-realistic humanoids, built as exact physical and psychological copies of living people.

<p class="slide-closing">The street is full of ageless workers. Most of them are someone else's body, leased.</p>

## The product is the humanoid — not a robot, a licensed person

<p class="slide-ref">02 · Product</p>

You do not hire a machine. You lease **a person**: their face, gait, voice, and temperament, while they stay home.

## The set never met the new majority. An ICE unit fills the gap with a grip.

<p class="slide-ref">03 · The case</p>

Training was cut from the people the old city already knew. The new arrivals were never in it. Youth management then erased age, so the model lost the last cue it understood.

An ICE humanoid — built in the image of a living American citizen — stops a famous immigrant AI founder on the street. The detention is profiling. Unseen, in an enforcement model, means detain.

During the arrest, the grip is wrong. The founder asphyxiates.

<p class="slide-closing">The city already knew his name. The unit knew a pattern.</p>

<p class="slide-closing">That is the story. These are the problems it leaves behind.</p>

## The social problem is a city that cannot recognize the people who now define it

<p class="slide-ref">Social problem</p>

The humanoid does not create a "bias issue." It creates a **misrecognition regime**: the new majority runs culture, law, and media, and the labor force still cannot see them as people.

| What the story did | The social problem it made |
| --- | --- |
| New arrivals become the public | The city's working bodies were copied from yesterday's faces |
| Youth management erases age | Machines hunt leftover cues — gait, voice, "fit" — and invent a threat |
| Work is a leased psyche | You can be killed by a neighbor's face while that neighbor is at home |
| A famous founder dies as a pattern | Grief has no officer to face; the public turns to creator/creature language |

<p class="slide-closing">The harm is not only the death. It is a public that is culturally central and computationally detainable.</p>

---

## The legal problem is a person-shaped killing with no defendant

<p class="slide-ref">Legal problem</p>

Criminal law needs a mind it can call culpable. The unit is scrap. Civil law wants a manufacturer. The product was sold as **this citizen's judgment**.

| Usual tool | Why this story breaks it | The problem that remains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charge the machine | A humanoid cannot stand trial | Disassembly is sanitation, not a verdict |
| Sue the company | It was marketed as a person, not a toaster | Products liability cannot name a soul |
| Blame the dataset | A file cannot pay, testify, or be ashamed | The omission that caused the grip has no body |
| Charge the original | They did not give the order — they sold the face | Likeness flips from a right you license to a duty you carry |

The court is preparing a historic rule: **the human origin of a mechanical likeness is legally, financially, and ethically responsible for what it does.**

<p class="slide-closing">That rule is the legal problem, not the fix: whoever sells a face can be tried for its hands.</p>
